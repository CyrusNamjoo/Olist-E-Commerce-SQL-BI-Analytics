# Memo: Unusual order activity, findings and recommended actions

**To:** Risk & Compliance Manager
**From:** Sirous Namjoo, BI Analyst
**Period:** 2 Jan 2017 – 26 Aug 2018 (86 weeks)
**Reports:** weekly unusual orders, by week, by seller region and by seller (see README)

## Summary

Over the period, **1,168 of 98,962 orders (1.2%)** were cancelled or could not be supplied. Customers had paid **R$254,700** on these orders. Most of the problem does not sit with individual sellers.

## Findings and recommended actions

**1. Most unusual orders (62%) can't be linked to any seller.**
Almost all of them are orders marked *unavailable*: the customer paid, but the product was never supplied. Customers paid **R$124,200** on unavailable orders. No seller ranking will ever show this group.
→ **Action:** confirm that these customers were refunded, and escalate to Operations to find out why products could be bought when there was no stock.

**2. One seller stands out in the latest weeks.**
In the 4 weeks to 26 Aug 2018, seller `81783131…` (São Paulo) had **5 unusual orders**. No other seller had more than 2.
→ **Action:** review this seller's 5 orders to find the cause before deciding whether to follow up.

**3. There's no regional hotspot.**
São Paulo sellers account for 73% of the unusual orders that can be traced to a seller, but they also handle 71% of all orders. Their share matches their size.
→ **Action:** none needed. Keep monitoring regions in the weekly report.

## What not to rely on yet

- **Refunds aren't recorded.** The data shows what customers paid on these orders, but not whether the money was returned. Finding 1 can't be closed until refund records are checked.
- **Recent weeks may go up later.** Orders are counted in the week they were bought, and orders bought recently can still be cancelled. Treat the last 2–3 weeks as provisional.
